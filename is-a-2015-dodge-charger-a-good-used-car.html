Vehicle Intelligence · 2015 Dodge Charger (7th Generation, LD)

Is a 2015 Dodge Charger a Good Used Car to Buy?

It depends — on the specific car, its recall status, and how it was treated. The 2015 Charger is a fast, roomy, characterful rear-drive sedan with genuinely appealing V8s, but it was a recall-heavy year with real reliability caveats. A well-kept, fully-recalled example can be a lot of car; a hard-used or neglected one is a gamble. So check it carefully by VIN and with a mechanic before you buy.

GOOD/BAD CAR FOR YOU

Why it cuts both ways

The 2015 Charger (the refreshed LD) is one of the few full-size rear-drive sedans left — roomy, comfortable, and available with a 3.6-liter V6, the 5.7-liter HEMI V8, and hotter 6.2 and 6.4 V8s. The character and the V8s are the appeal. The catch is that this model year drew a lot of recalls and some real reliability complaints, so the individual car matters a great deal.

What to know before you buy

  • Recall-heavy year The 2015 Charger had a string of recalls — driver and passenger air-bag inflators, a large alternator recall, a catalytic-converter item, front wheel-hub and spindle-nut issues, and a cruise-control item that could keep it from disengaging. Confirming these are done on the specific VIN is central.
  • Engine and cooling items Owner reports include water-pump failure (squealing, leaks) and exhaust-manifold cracks (a high-pitched noise under hard acceleration); check for both.
  • How it was driven A V8 Charger can be tempting to drive hard — look for signs of abuse, and prize a documented maintenance record.

Open recalls — this one has a lot

Given the number of campaigns on this car, the recall check is not a formality. Look up the exact car's open recalls free by VIN at NHTSA; a Dodge dealer completes any open recall at no charge — pay particular attention to the air-bag-inflator and alternator recalls.

What a history report tells you — and what it doesn't

On a car like this, the individual car's history is most of the decision. A history report is a record of what got reported — title brands, reported accidents and damage, odometer readings over time, and service records where they exist. That is what separates a clean, maintained example from a salvage-title or flood car wearing the same badge.

What a history report is not is a forecast, and an absence of records is not proof of a clean past — plenty happens to a car that never gets reported. That is why the honest answer is always the pair: the documented history and an in-person inspection by a mechanic you trust, on the specific car.

The things to check before you buy

  • Recall status Confirm the many possible recalls — air bags, alternator, catalytic converter, wheel hubs, cruise control — are completed on this VIN.
  • Water pump and exhaust manifolds Listen for squealing or leaks and a high-pitched noise under acceleration.
  • Signs of hard use Especially on V8 cars; look for tire, brake, and drivetrain abuse.
  • Maintenance history A documented service record on a performance sedan matters.
  • Accident and rust The usual panel and underbody checks.

What would make you walk

  • Uncompleted safety recalls the seller won't resolve Especially the air-bag-inflator and alternator items.
  • A hard-used V8 with no records Signs of abuse and no maintenance history.
  • Water-pump or exhaust-manifold symptoms with no repair plan Squealing, leaks, or a high-pitched exhaust tick.
  • Structural damage or rust The usual deal-breakers.
